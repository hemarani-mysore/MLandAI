# Due-Diligence Memo — Brightloom Foods

**Recommendation:** `proceed_with_conditions`  ·  **Confidence:** 65%

## Summary

Brightloom is an early-revenue plant-based ingredient manufacturer with real
patented technology and three anchor grocery customers, but two structural
risks — single-facility production and a meaningful growth deceleration —
should be resolved or explained before a full commitment.

## Overview

Brightloom Foods (founded 2018, Denver CO) manufactures plant-based protein
ingredients via a proprietary fermentation process, sold B2B to food
manufacturers and grocery private-label programs.

> [brightloom-overview] Brightloom Foods Company Overview — company-overview.md: "Brightloom Foods manufactures plant-based protein ingredients using a proprietary fermentation process, sold B2B to food manufacturers and grocery private-label programs."

## Market & Competition

Three major regional grocery chains are direct customers, indicating real
commercial validation in a competitive ingredient category, though the source
material does not detail the competitive landscape or Brightloom's relative
positioning against other fermentation-based protein makers.

> [brightloom-overview] Brightloom Foods Company Overview — company-overview.md: "Three major regional grocery chains as direct customers."

## Team & Funding

$22M raised across seed and Series A. Founding team pairs food-science and
industrial-fermentation backgrounds directly relevant to the core product.

> [brightloom-overview] Brightloom Foods Company Overview — company-overview.md: "CEO and CTO are co-founders with backgrounds in food science and industrial fermentation, respectively."

## Technology & IP

Two issued patents on the core fermentation process provide some defensibility,
though all production depends on a single facility with no second site yet
funded.

> [brightloom-overview] Brightloom Foods Company Overview — company-overview.md: "Two issued patents on the core fermentation process. All production currently runs through the single Colorado facility; a second facility is in early site-selection stages but unfunded."

## Traction & Financials

$9M revenue growing 28% YoY, a real deceleration from 52% the prior year. The
company attributes this to one large customer's order volumes normalizing
after an initial stocking bulge — a plausible explanation, but one that
should be validated against order data from the other two grocery accounts
before being taken at face value.

> [brightloom-overview] Brightloom Foods Company Overview — company-overview.md: "$9M trailing-twelve-month revenue, growing 28% year over year — down from 52% growth the prior year, a deceleration the company attributes to one large retail customer normalizing order volumes after an initial stocking bulge."

## Risks & Red Flags

Single-facility production concentration is a real operational risk with no
funded mitigation yet in place, and the growth deceleration — while plausibly
explained — has not been independently corroborated across the full customer
base.

> [brightloom-overview] Brightloom Foods Company Overview — company-overview.md: "Single-facility production concentration (no manufacturing redundancy if the facility is disrupted); year-over-year growth deceleration from 52% to 28%."

## Open Questions

- Do order volumes at the other two grocery accounts corroborate the "stocking bulge" explanation for decelerating growth?
- What is the funding timeline and cost for a second production facility?
